Visual identification

Use the photographs as a comparison set. Look for the same feature on your tree rather than matching the whole-tree silhouette.

Leaf Check simple/compound, opposite/alternate, leaflet number, size, texture, hairs and underside colour.
Bark Check colour, fissures, flakes, papery bark, thorns and any resin or latex.
Fruit Check pod, berry, capsule, winged fruit and whether fruit hangs from the twig, trunk or branch.
Habitat Check granite, sand, clay, river margin, mopane woodland, miombo or teak woodland.

Field checklist

When identifying an unknown tree, record these before choosing a name:
1. LeavesSimple or compound? Opposite or alternate? Shape, size, smell, hairs, glands.
2. StemThorns, prickles, spines, glands, exudate and bark texture.
3. FruitShape, wings, pods, berries, capsules and where attached.
4. PlaceSoil, altitude, rainfall, woodland type, rocks and proximity to water.

Photographs

For identification, compare the leaf and twig first, then bark and fruit. A whole-tree photograph alone is rarely enough.

Photographs are loaded from their original source when internet is available. The guide itself works offline, but remote photographs may not appear without connectivity. Zimbabwe seasons

Early rains Many deciduous trees leaf out rapidly; watch for fresh flushes and first flowers.
Summer Flowering and fruiting are highly species- and rainfall-dependent. Record both leaf and reproductive characters.
Late rains Pods, capsules and winged fruits can become the easiest identification characters.
Dry season Bark, thorns, branching pattern and persistent fruit become especially important.

Seasonal timing varies with rainfall, altitude and locality. Species-level flowering months will be added only where supported by the botanical record.

About this guide
This is an original field key built around observable characters—leaf arrangement and form, thorns, bark, fruit and habitat. It is designed to complement, not reproduce, Meg Coates Palgrave's copyrighted keys or text. Her Key to the Trees of Zimbabwe was published in 1996 and covered six Zimbabwe areas; her work remains an important foundation for Zimbabwe tree identification.

Useful references: Flora of Zimbabwe · Key to Trees of Southern Africa
